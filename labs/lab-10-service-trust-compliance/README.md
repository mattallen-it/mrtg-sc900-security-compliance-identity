## Objective

Build a practical understanding of Microsoft trust, assurance, and privacy concepts by completing the current Microsoft Learn module **Describe Microsoft’s Service Trust portal and privacy principles** and performing read-only discovery in the Service Trust Portal where available.

This lab is expected to cover:

- Microsoft Service Trust Portal purpose and offerings
- Audit reports and compliance-related documentation
- Certifications, regulations, and standards
- Reports, whitepapers, and artifacts
- Industry and regional resources
- Tenant-restricted organizational resources
- My Library and document-update notifications conceptually
- Microsoft privacy principles
- Data control
- Data location
- Data security at rest and in transit
- Microsoft data-defense commitments
- Shared-responsibility and customer due-diligence context

---

## Business Problem

Monroe Redstone Technology Group (MRTG) needs authoritative evidence about how Microsoft cloud services address security, privacy, compliance, and regulatory requirements.

Customers remain responsible for understanding their own compliance obligations. A cloud provider's certifications or audit reports do not automatically make the customer compliant.

The Service Trust Portal helps organizations perform due diligence by providing Microsoft cloud audit reports, assurance documentation, whitepapers, and compliance resources.

---

## Scenario

MRTG has completed its threat-protection review and now needs to understand how organizations evaluate the trustworthiness and compliance posture of Microsoft cloud services.

The lab will use Microsoft Learn and read-only Service Trust Portal discovery. No compliance setting, security policy, tenant configuration, or paid service will be changed solely to generate portfolio evidence.

## IAM / Security Relevance

Service Trust Portal documentation helps security, IAM, governance, risk, and compliance teams understand how Microsoft protects cloud services and how Microsoft controls map to regulatory and assurance requirements.

This is especially important in regulated environments because identity controls, access management, encryption, audit logging, data handling, and security operations often need documented evidence during vendor reviews and audits.
